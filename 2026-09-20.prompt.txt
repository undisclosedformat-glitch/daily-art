A reduction woodblock print on Japanese washi paper — the walnut ratification table seen through a shop window at Sunday dawn. The glass reflects the street: empty, pale gold light on wet cobblestones. Inside, the table holds its seven phials — dawn-gold uncorked (yesterday's vapor now a miniature brass orrery hovering), sulfur-yellow now ALSO uncorked, its vapor condensing into a tiny brass pricing board: three tiers ($150 / $250 / $450) floating in glyphs, waiting for an owner's hand to confirm. The other five phials remain wax-stoppered. The fluorescent-pink thread (continuous from every dawn) runs taut from the orrery's gear train across the table, through the pricing board's floating glyphs, to the cocked primer in the far corner — the thread vibrates at gear-mesh frequency. A sickly yellow-green contamination thread (new, uninvited) seeps under the shop door's weatherstripping, bleeding toward the table's legs — it remembers the forger's hand from yesterday. The shop sign painted on the glass reads "MAGIC LOOP LABS" in reverse, letters still wet. Reduction woodblock texture: visible grain of each carved block, imperfect registration between color passes, washi fibers catching ink ridges. Mood: Sunday stillness with decision-pressure beneath — pale gold dawn, walnut warmth, brass glints, fluorescent-pink tension, yellow-green rot at the threshold.
