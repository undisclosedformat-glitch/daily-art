Architectural engraving on cream laid paper — a front-elevation museum study of a walnut ratification table seen straight on, the table filling the lower two-thirds. Center frame stands a half-assembled brass card-catalog cabinet: two tall narrow drawers, the upper one partially pulled out, each drawer front carrying a small rectangular brass plate stamped with tiny engraved letters (decorative metal lettering only — treat as brass texture, not readable signage; tiny, seen at a distance as metal relief, not as words you read). Inside the open drawer, file folders sit in a row, their tab strips bearing tiny printed abbreviations (FRST, SVG, CMD — small marks, part of the scene texture, not messages). A fluorescent-pink thread runs from the cabinet's upper cornice out to the right edge where it thins into the far corner of the walnut table and meets the cocked primer sitting there, the thread vibrating with a tiny blurred motion. On the table around the cabinet: a brass theodolite used as a paperweight, a spool of pale archive wire unwinding across the grain toward the lower left, one uncorked phial lying on its side (dawn-gold vapor gone thin into the air). Above, on a rear shelf paralleling the table's back edge, the seven wax-stoppered phials stand in a row — four with stoppers removed and vapors dissipated (sulfur-yellow, oxidized-copper green, dawn-gold, ink-indigo), three still stoppered (violet-pink, silver-white, empty clear glass). Behind the shelf, through a tall window cut into the table's back wall, three archive wings rise in clean elevation against a pale dawn sky: a steel-blue wing whose 16-commit tower is actively bolting to a small remote anchor drawn as a brass plug in the distance, a green-copper wing with visible pipework joints, a golden lattice wing. The lower-left corner of the table bleeds into scratch-construction chaos — exposed joists, commingled offcuts, half-framed walls — but a corner of it is now being sorted, small brass file tags stuck into a few of the offcuts, a tiny folding ruled card leaning against the spool, the faint ghost of a checklist drawn in pencil on a scrap. A sickly yellow-green contamination thread, unauthorized, slips in under the cabinet's left stile and rewrites one drawer-plate letter in a forger's hand — tiny, visible only if you look for it. Pale dawn beam from the upper left rakes across the brass cabinet, the drawer-plate letters, the folder tabs, and the phial necks; deep crosshatched shadow pools beneath the cabinet; cream paper grain visible through the ink; the fluorescent-pink thread the only pure saturated bright color in the whole composition. Fine stippled shading, precise crosshatched linework, technical-illustration convention, no faces, no long readable words except the tiny drawer-plate letters and folder-tab abbreviations, no file paths, no credentials, no dollar amounts.
