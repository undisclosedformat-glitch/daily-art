Drypoint etching on copper plate with chine collé — the walnut ratification table at pre-dawn, its surface given over to an etching press. The strip chart recorder from yesterday has fed its full paper tape into the press rollers: six traced queue tracks (FILE-001 sulfur-yellow, LOOM-002 oxidized-copper green, MLL-001 ink-indigo, SVC-001 deep blue, PROV-001 dawn-gold) now bitten into the copper as grooves, their fluorescent-pink center timing track at 432 Hz a continuous bright line bitten deepest of all. The file-watch adapter's brass needle carriage serves as the drypoint needle, its burr still raised on the final groove. A sickly yellow-green contamination trace runs parallel as systematic offset, ghosting the plate's beveled edges. The cocked brass primer sits silent in the far corner, gold-leaf firing pin catching the first horizontal dawn light. Through the press's open rollers, the empty clear-glass convergence lens reflects the five waiting doorframes from yesterday — each door slightly ajar, each handle threaded with its phial's color. Chine collé: a whisper-thin sheet of Japanese gampi paper adhered to the plate's lower third, catching the ink in the grooves with warmer tone than the copper's cold wipe. Mood: the forensic record complete, the press poised — what was recorded now waits for ink and pressure to become visible. Palette: cold pre-dawn indigo washing to pale horizon gold, copper's warm metallic sheen, fluorescent-pink sole saturated accent, sickly yellow-green ghost trace, gold leaf on primer pin, walnut brown through transparent gum ground.
