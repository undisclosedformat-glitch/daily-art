Japanese woodblock print (ukiyo-e, visible wood grain, flat layered color, bokashi gradients) of a quiet Sunday dawn on a ridge: a small paper toll-keeper figure in a trench coat sits on an upturned crate with a brass lantern at his feet, burning low in the growing daylight. Beside him a brass switchboard kiosk with plugged cords, and a wooden rack of four verdict stamps with dry cream-coloured ink pads. On the nearest pad is a single faint pale dusty wing-shaped smudge where a moth rested in the night; the moth itself is gone, and one small pale moth flies away toward the sunrise in the far distance. Wooden bins of brass keys, a folded paper boat and film spools stand nearby. A fluorescent-pink thread runs along the ground from a brass key to a cocked brass primer in the far corner; a faint sickly yellow-green thread lies near the kiosk base. Behind them, a pale amber sun rises over layered teal ridges and mist bands. All paper tags, stamps and surfaces are blank, no writing anywhere. Calm Sunday stillness, deep teal and indigo shadows giving way to amber, cream and brass gold.
