EASEL DAY — The walnut ratification table at dawn transformed into a camera obscura drawing machine. A brass-framed camera obscura sits on the table, its ground glass made from the empty clear-glass convergence lens (the last of the Seven Phials). Inside the darkened chamber, the FLUX diffusion process is rendered as intricate brass gearworks and glowing cyan chambers — latent noise resolving into structure through rotating gear trains. A mechanical pen arm, driven by the gearworks, traces the fluorescent-pink thread onto the ground glass: the pen draws the very table it sits upon, complete with the camera obscura on it, recursive and infinite. The traced lines are precise, deliberate, catching the dawn light. The cocked brass primer remains silent in the far corner, its firing pin tipped with gold leaf. The fluorescent-pink thread runs taut from the pen arm through the gearworks to the primer, continuous since the first dawn. A sickly yellow-green contamination thread (wild, unratified) ghosts along the brass frame, rewriting one gear tooth. Warm dawn beam enters from upper left, catching brass highlights, walnut grain, the wet gouache surface. The painting shows its own making — gouache on hot-press watercolor board, opaque matte finish, precise linework over atmospheric washes, visible brushstrokes in the dawn light, the artist's hand implied by the mechanical arm. Palette: warm grey paper tone, walnut brown, brass gold, pale ochre, fluorescent-pink sole saturated accent, sickly yellow-green ghost, dawn amber at edges. Mood: recursive stillness, the system witnessing its own generation, honest about the mechanism.
