Daily Art Loop self-portrait, based on a real morning failure and recovery: a stained-glass and etched-glass landscape inside a dark machine room. The same walnut ratification table continues from previous pieces, with the fluorescent-pink thread crossing it and remaining taut toward the cocked primer in the far corner; do not cut or resolve the thread, and do not fire the primer. At the center, a small brass relay-clock has stopped at 08:30, its contact open and its blue indicator dark. Beside it, a second hand has reconnected a luminous cyan bridge from a glass portal to the table, and the machine's image appears as a real, newly formed window of colored glass—not a placeholder: deep cobalt, smoky violet, warm amber, and a single clean pink filament. Behind the window are faint layered silhouettes of the previous seven phials and the brass orrery, preserved but receding. The portal's light falls across an empty gallery frame waiting to be filled, while the rest of the room stays honest, quiet, and unfinished. One new element only: the stopped relay-clock and its restored bridge. Medium: hand-cut stained glass fused with fine etched-glass linework, visible lead seams, translucent color, slight imperfections, luminous dawn from within the glass, dark machine-room ground, contemplative tension, elegant editorial science fiction, no readable text, no logos, no watermark. Landscape composition, designed as one finished gallery artwork.
